01

What is there now, and what is wrong with it

Read from the repo, not guessed: src/app, src/components, globals.css, lib/graph.

The system already has a real point of view — brutalist paper-and-ink, hard borders, offset shadows, one red accent, Bloom's six colours as the only chromatic vocabulary, radius locked to 0px. That is a genuine identity and most of this rubric builds on it rather than throwing it away. What it lacks is depth, continuity and reward: almost every state change is instant, nothing carries the eye from one page to the next, and the one big animated element (the WebGL graph) is doing decorative work on a page where nobody is looking at it.

05

The motion system

Four curves, five durations. If a transition is not on this table it does not ship. This is what makes a heavily animated site feel designed rather than busy.

Easing curves — plotted livelive
Duration ladder
TokenmsUsed for
--snap90Button press, checkbox, field focus. Anything the finger caused.
--tick160Hover, tooltip, colour change.
--reveal320Card entrance, accordion, toast.
--swim640Modal, drawer, page transition, stage change.
--epic1200+Login background beats, hero morphs. Scroll-driven only — never time-driven.

Choreography rule: a group of elements entering together staggers at index × 45ms, capped at 6 items. Beyond 6, the group animates as one block. This single rule is what stops long lists from feeling like a slot machine.

Reversibility rule: anything scroll-driven must be a pure function of scroll position — no velocity integration, no state. Scroll up and it runs exactly backwards. The current GraphEngine already gets this right; keep it.

Heavy motion

Landing hero, login background, stage transitions, results reveal, empty states. Places where the user is arriving, waiting, or being rewarded.

Medium motion

Sidebar, cards, toasts, chat, command palette. Motion confirms cause and effect and nothing more.

Near-zero motion

Settings, API keys, the extraction editor, any table of real data. Work surfaces. Motion here is a tax on the user.

Route-level loading

Replace the three identical loading.tsx files with a single skeleton that matches the shape of the page it is replacing — a history skeleton looks like history rows, an agents skeleton looks like agent cards. A skeleton that mimics the real layout removes the layout shift and the perceived wait at once.

Top-of-page 2px ink bar (--accent) for any navigation under 400ms; full skeleton beyond that. Never both.

First-paint / app boot

A one-time boot sequence, ~900ms max, that draws the wordmark as line art then fills it — using the same stroke-draw machinery as the login background, so the brand's first frame and the login's first frame are the same gesture. Skipped entirely on repeat visits within the session and under prefers-reduced-motion.

Budget: the boot animation must never block interaction. It plays over a live page, not in front of it.

12

Data & diagram components

The components that carry the actual product: the Bloom wheel, stat readouts, the question grid, the extraction view.

Bloom wheel — hover a segmentlive
Specification

Bloom wheel. Six arcs sized by question count. On load each arc sweeps in from 0° at 90ms stagger. Hover lifts the arc 6px outward, dims the rest to 25%, and streams its label in. Click filters the results grid — the wheel is a control, not a chart.

Stat readouts. Every number is an odometer that counts up once on first view (IntersectionObserver, never re-runs). Tabular numerals mandatory.

Question grid. Masonry of question cards, each with its Bloom pip, confidence bar and verify stamp. New questions arrive with a 45ms stagger and a stamp landing. Filtering animates with FLIP so cards move to their new positions rather than re-appearing.

Extraction view. The parsed diagram is drawn as an interactive node-edge overlay on top of the user's own image — nodes fade in one by one as they are found, edges draw between them. This is the single most convincing moment in the product and it currently has no animation at all.

Empty states. Every empty list gets line art + one Caveat aside + one primary action. Never an empty box.

13

★ Replacing the graph behind the login

The brief's central question. Three complete answers, each running live below, each scroll-scrubbed inside its own frame. Scroll inside a frame and the background advances exactly as it would on the real page.

Why the current one has to go

Today /login mounts the landing page's GraphEngine with fixedBeat={3} at 38% opacity. Beat 3 is "collapse into the wordmark" — the end of the landing narrative. So the login page shows the last frame of a story the visitor may never have seen, frozen, dimmed to the point of illegibility, behind an opaque card that covers its centre.

  • It doesn't move. A fixed beat means the only motion is a 1.6px idle wobble. On the highest-intent page in the product.
  • It doesn't mean anything here. A generic node-and-edge cloud is the single most over-used "AI" background on the internet. It says nothing about diagrams, about Bloom levels, or about teaching.
  • It's illegible. At 38% opacity, behind a card with a 14px hard shadow, on a textured paper ground, you cannot read the structure — so all the WebGL cost buys texture.
  • It doesn't respond. Not to the pointer, not to the form, not to success or failure. It is wallpaper with a GPU context.

The test every replacement must pass: could a teacher who has never seen the marketing site look at this background for four seconds and understand what the product does?

Shared principles for all three
Scroll is the timelineThe login page becomes ~2.5 viewports tall instead of one. The card is position:sticky and never leaves; the background advances through beats. You can sign in without scrolling a pixel — scrolling is a reward, not a requirement.
Pure function of scrollSame discipline as the current engine: no velocity integration, no accumulated state. Scroll up and it runs exactly backwards; stop and it holds.
It reacts to the formFocus a field and the background responds. Submit and it resolves. Fail and it recoils. The background is part of the sign-in, not behind it.
Legibility firstA contrast guard keeps a clear zone behind the card: the background's own opacity drops locally wherever the card sits. No text ever competes with artwork.
Graceful floorReduced motion → the final formation, static, at full quality. No WebGL → SVG/canvas-2D fallback of the same drawing. Low memory → fewer elements, same composition.
Cost ceiling≤1 canvas, ≤2 draw calls or ≤120 SVG nodes, ≤3ms/frame on a 2019 laptop, paused via IntersectionObserver, dropped entirely after successful auth.

Interaction & response

Pointer: the plate has three depth layers (outline / organelles / labels) that parallax ≤7px. Restrained — depth, not sliding.

Field focus: focusing email dims the plate to 55% and pulls a faint spotlight behind the card; focusing password draws a dashed "security" ring around the plate that closes as you type (one dash per character, capped).

How it works

Structure. Six translucent sheets stacked in Z, each carrying a fragment of the same diagram — the outline on sheet 1, the connections on sheet 3, the annotations on sheet 6. Only when all six are aligned do you see the complete picture. That is Bloom's taxonomy as an image: the same subject understood at six depths.

Scroll. Scroll drives camera Z. Sheets rush toward the lens, pass through it and dissolve. As each sheet crosses the focal plane it flares in its Bloom colour and prints its level name at the edge. The bottom of the page is sheet 6 — Create — and the deepest sheet is the wordmark plate.

Light. A soft light source sits behind the stack, so the sheets read as backlit acetate: bright where they overlap, dark where they do not. That single lighting idea is what makes it look expensive rather than merely 3D.

Pointer. The stack yaws ±6° toward the cursor and the sheets shear at different rates, producing genuine parallax between layers.

Success. On submit, all six sheets snap into perfect alignment — the complete diagram resolves for the first time — and the whole stack whites out into the console. Failure scatters the alignment by a few degrees and rings the frame red.

Engineering notes

This is the one proposal that justifies WebGL, and it reuses the existing engine's discipline: one instanced draw for the sheet geometry, one for the glyph lines, all beat interpolation in the vertex shader, beat written to a module singleton from a single rAF, zero React re-renders. The existing GraphEngine already has every one of these patterns — this replaces its content, not its architecture.

Budget: 2 draw calls, ~1.5k vertices, no post-processing, DPR capped at 1.5, powerPreference:'low-power', paused off-screen. Fallback: a canvas-2D version (what you see running above) at 6 sheets, then a static SVG of the aligned stack.

Trade-off: requires the dark palette to work. On the paper theme, backlit acetate has nothing to be backlit against. If you want light mode to stay primary, take proposal A.

Reactive behaviour try it above

Idle: rings rotate at their own rates, slowly, forever. Ring 1 fastest, ring 6 slowest — like a real orrery, the outer bodies are the slow ones.

Email focused: the instrument leans in and the rings gather — orbital eccentricity drops, everything aligns to a single meridian. It is visibly paying attention.

Typing the password: the node glyphs scramble character by character and the rings counter-rotate into a lock; one node per character clicks into the meridian. When the field is long enough the mechanism visibly seats.

Success: the rings spin up, the core flares, and the whole instrument collapses inward into the loading state of /app — one continuous move across the route change.

Failure: the mechanism jams — rings stop hard, counter-rotate 8°, and one node drops out of orbit and falls. No red, no shake; a machine failing is more legible than an error colour.

Cost: canvas-2D is enough (that is what is running above) — 6 rings × 14 nodes = 84 primitives, ~0.8ms/frame. WebGL optional, not required.

Direct answers to the brief

How is it animated?Two independent layers. Layout & grid

The landing page and the console currently use two unrelated layout systems. One system, two densities.

The grid
Columns12 at ≥1280, 8 at ≥900, 4 below. Gutter 24px, margin clamp(20px, 4vw, 64px).
Max width1240px for marketing, full-bleed for the console. Reading columns never exceed 66ch regardless.
Spacing scale4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 36 · 56 · 88. Eight steps, no exceptions.
DensityMarketing = comfortable (56px section padding). Console = compact (24px). One token flips it.
RulesSection boundaries are ink rules, not whitespace — that is what makes it read as a document. Rules draw in left-to-right on first view.
Breakpoint behaviourThe console rail collapses at 1080 and becomes a sheet at 768. The login card centres and the background drops to its 2-beat mobile variant at 640.
Page-transition continuity

The single biggest perceptual upgrade available, and the cheapest: give three elements stable identities across routes — the wordmark, the background object, and the primary card. Then landing → login → console is one continuous move instead of three page loads.

Implementation: React 19 + Next 16 supports the View Transitions API directly; the existing PageTransition wrapper becomes the place to opt routes in with view-transition-name. Fallback for unsupported browsers is the current wipe mask — which already exists in globals.css.

Exit transitions must be under 200ms. A slow exit makes a fast app feel slow; a slow entrance makes it feel considered.

Accessibility & performance budget

A fully animated site is only defensible if it degrades perfectly and costs nothing.

Accessibility
Reduced motionEvery scroll-driven background renders its final formation, statically. Every entrance becomes an opacity fade ≤120ms. No parallax, no auto-play, no grain. This is already partly wired in globals.css — extend it to cover all new work.
FocusVisible on every interactive element in every track. Focus order follows visual order. The flipped login face must be inert, not just aria-hidden.
Contrast4.5:1 body, 3:1 large. Bloom 03 (#c6f24e) fails on paper at small sizes — it is already darkened in this document to #a8d825; do the same in the app or restrict it to fills with dark text.
Motion & meaningNo information exists only in motion. Stage state is a dot colour and a label. A flagged question is red and carries the word FLAGGED.
CanvasAll decorative canvases aria-hidden + pointer-events:none. Never focusable, never announced.
Screen readerStage advances announce via a polite live region. Streaming chat announces on completion, not per token.
Performance budget
Background≤3ms/frame · ≤2 draw calls · DPR ≤1.5 · low-power · IntersectionObserver pause · destroyed on route leave.
Scroll handlersExactly one rAF per page. Scroll writes to a module singleton; nothing calls setState per frame. The current Landing.tsx and graphBeat already do this correctly — that pattern is the house rule.
Animation propertiestransform and opacity only on anything running per-frame. clip-path and stroke-dashoffset permitted on discrete, short animations.
FontsFour families stays the ceiling. display:swap, subset to latin, preconnect. Track C adds Instrument Serif and must drop a weight elsewhere.
TargetsLCP <2.0s · CLS <0.02 (skeletons must match final layout) · INP <150ms · main-thread idle within 300ms of first scroll.
AssetsZero decorative raster. The 28MB of SVGs in /images stay out of the critical path — sample or reference them lazily, never ship them to first paint.
